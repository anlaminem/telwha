Behavioral Market Simulation

One engine, four scenarios. I tested how the same market behaves across calm, bubble, crash, and stressed regimes.

The idea is to look beyond price alone. Volatility and drawdown need to be read alongside it, because they show where the market is simply moving and where it is already starting to pressure the person, the product, and the risk layer.

What I built I created one readable simulator with 4 regimes and 3 core visual layers.
Why it matters The same system can look calm, overheated, or painful depending on how stress is structured.
What I found A similar ending price does not mean the internal regime was the same.

What it is

This is a market simulation for one asset across four scenarios. In each one, I look at three simple layers — price, realized volatility, and drawdown from the peak.

Who it is for

For a trader, it is a way to see the difference between price movement and stress. For AI, fintech, and product teams, it is an example of how to explain a market regime clearly, not only technically.

Scenarios

Calm

A stable background: a narrow range, low volatility, and the smallest drawdown.

Bubble

A smooth acceleration upward: the picture still looks attractive, but fragility is already building underneath.

Crash

A short, sharp удар: volatility jumps the most, and stress arrives fast.

Stressed

Not an instant catastrophe, but a long uncomfortable regime where drawdown hurts for the longest time.

How to read

The same data is shown through three layers. They help distinguish between “the market is simply moving” and “the market is psychologically under pressure.”

Price

The top layer is the price trajectory. What matters is that several scenarios end close to one another, yet looked completely different inside.

Volatility

The middle layer shows where tension actually rises. Bubble and crash both produce high volatility, but with different profiles: gradual heating versus a short shock.

Drawdown

The bottom layer is drawdown from the previous peak. This is where it becomes clear that the stressed regime hurts longer, even if the drop is not as abrupt as in crash.

Findings

1

The same engine can produce very different regimes. The formula is the same, but market behavior changes once the agent mix and the shock structure change.

2

Price alone is not enough. Two scenarios can end at a similar price level and still look completely different in volatility and drawdown.

3

Bubble, crash, and stressed differ by more than direction. They differ in how stress builds up and reveals itself inside the trajectory.

4

The hardest regime is not always the one with the loudest fall. A long, sticky drawdown can put more psychological pressure than a short shock.

5

Calm is a regime where discomfort stays limited and the system returns to balance faster, so the same price movement feels much easier to process.

Implications

Behavioral Finance

The market should be read as a regime, not only as a return series. Stress, memory of the peak, and duration of pain are more visible through volatility and drawdown than through the price chart alone.

FinTech / Broker / Risk

It is important to see more than the current price. A stressed regime may be more dangerous for retention than a short crash, because it wears people down longer and pushes them toward worse decisions.

Communication / Product

It is easier to explain not only the market direction, but also the type of tension inside it. A simple trio of price, volatility, and drawdown already creates a clear story that can be shown in a product interface or user communication.

Technical Note

A short note on the MVP boundaries, without unnecessary technical weight.

What is included

  • Bounded MVP: one asset, four scenarios, simple price formation.
  • Series for price, realized volatility, and drawdown.
  • A readable case page with three static comparative charts.

What is excluded and what comes next

  • No full order-book microstructure, multi-asset contagion, or complex execution logic.
  • The next step is richer agent views, pattern tagging, calibration, and links to real market data.
